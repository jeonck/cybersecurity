## What this is

This knowledge base has two layers. The first eight domains collect the templates, trackers, logs, and policy documents a security team reaches for on a regular basis; each of those also carries added concept-reference pages for deeper background. The last three domains are pure concept-reference libraries with no document templates of their own.

**Templates + concepts:**

1. [Information Security](/docs/information-security/) — access rights, data classification, breach notification, DLP, retention, KPIs, plus cryptography and CIA-triad fundamentals
2. [Network Security](/docs/network-security/) — DDoS mitigation, IP allow/deny lists, access control logging, device inventory, traffic monitoring, plus zero trust, SASE/SDP, and protocol security concepts
3. [Cloud Security](/docs/cloud-security/) — access control, asset inventory, backup/recovery testing, incident logging, configuration baselines, plus CASB/CNAPP/CSPM and Kubernetes security concepts
4. [Application Security](/docs/application-security/) — patch tracking, secure coding, mobile app testing, misconfiguration logs, static analysis, threat modeling, plus OWASP-class vulnerability and DevSecOps concepts
5. [Security Management](/docs/security-management/) — acceptable use, password, backup, disposal, classification, transfer, and ISMS policies, plus ISO 27001/COBIT and privacy regulation concepts
6. [Incident Management](/docs/incident-management/) — incident policy/process, major and intern incident reports, structural and workplace incident reports, plus SIEM/EDR and digital forensics concepts
7. [Problem Management](/docs/problem-management/) — known error records, major problem reports, problem management process
8. [Disaster Recovery](/docs/disaster-recovery/) — DR approach, asset register, closure reports, communications plan, DR plan template

**Concept references only:**

9. [Identity & Access Management](/docs/identity-access-management/) — OAuth, OIDC, SAML, SSO, Kerberos, FIDO, passkeys, and directory federation
10. [Offensive Security](/docs/offensive-security/) — penetration testing methodology, OSINT, fuzzing, red team and CTF concepts
11. [Emerging Security & Technology](/docs/emerging-security/) — AI/ML and LLM security, deepfakes, IoT/OT, and blockchain

## How each page is structured

Every page follows the same three-part structure so you can scan quickly. Document-template pages use:

- **I. Overview** — what the document is, and why a security program needs it
- **II. Structure & Process** — the fields/sections the document should contain and the workflow around it, shown as a diagram
- **III. Best Practices & Comparison** — maturity guidance and how the document relates to neighbouring templates or standards

Concept-reference pages use the same visual language with headings suited to a technical concept:

- **I. Overview** — a definition and the key features of the concept
- **II. Mechanism & Components** — how it works, shown as a diagram, and its key components
- **III. Advanced Topics & Comparison** — how it compares to related concepts, and current trends
