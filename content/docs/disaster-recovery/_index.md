Disaster recovery covers the planning, testing, and execution work needed to restore IT and security capability after a disruptive event — a data center outage, ransomware attack, natural disaster, or major infrastructure failure. It is the operational arm of business continuity, translating recovery time and recovery point targets into concrete plans, roles, and runbooks that teams can execute under pressure rather than improvise during a crisis.

These documents are typically owned by a DR coordinator or business continuity manager, working closely with IT infrastructure, application, and security teams who hold the technical recovery responsibilities. Together they keep recovery strategy, asset dependencies, and communication plans current and exercised so that an actual disaster is the first time they are tested only in the sense that stakes are real — not in the sense that the plan is unproven.
